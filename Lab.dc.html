Collaborative Human-AI Learning Ecologies for STEM+C Lab

The CoHALE Lab designs and studies collaborative human-AI learning ecologies for STEM and computing. We build learning environments in which students, teachers, and AI systems work on disciplinary problems together – sensor-based inquiry in school gardens, generative-AI partners in teachers' professional learning, XR maker experiences in teacher preparation – study them in classrooms and courses with partner teachers, and build theory about what an AI participant must carry, and what must stay with people, for that work to hold up after the research team leaves.

Three research strands

Disciplinary computational practicesComputational thinking, data practice, and AI literacy learned inside a subject.
High-quality collaborationWhether students, teachers, and AI partners can recognize and improve their joint work.
AI and emerging technologies as participantsWhat a technology must carry, and what must stay with people.

How we work

Co-design with teachers
Partner teachers co-design the units and modules they will teach and take ownership of them as a project matures.
Design-based research in real settings
Iterative cycles in classrooms, courses, and professional learning, with mixed methods – surveys, student and teacher artifacts, classroom video, AI interaction logs, interviews.
Artifacts that scaffold and measure
Competence models built into rubrics, matrices, and AI mentors that both guide the work and make it observable.

Projects

NSF CAREER2026–2031 · PI
3AforCT. Sensor-based school-garden inquiry in two rural Texas districts, carrying data practices across the inquiry cycle; an AI peer mentor that models expert reasoning and fades; teachers who co-design the unit they teach. Built on two pilot grants (2024–2026): AIM4Rural (Emerge, $49K) and AI-Enhanced CT & Agricultural Science (Catapult, $30K).
NSF IUSE2026–2031 · Co-PI
AI-Driven Professional Learning. A generative-AI cognitive partner that scaffolds preservice teachers through creativity-directed mathematics tasks, implemented across eight teacher preparation programs.
Design studiesongoing
Computing and AI in teacher preparation. XR maker experiences and AI-literacy modules through which preservice teachers take up computational and AI practices inside their own subjects, working with and supported by the technologies they will teach with. Recent work: a diagnostic framework for reading makerspace process data as evidence of computational thinking (Interactive Learning Environments, 2026); safeguards and professional learning needs for AI integration (Cogent Education, 2026).
CSCL lineongoing
Collaborative competence and the technologies that support it. Whether teams can recognize and improve their own joint work – from a system that returned to student groups the criteria their discussions were judged by, to a chat-based simulation partner for practicing collective sensemaking – and what models of collaborative quality miss when the talk turns to identity and difference.
