About

I am an Assistant Professor of STEM Education (Technology Education emphasis) in the Department of Teaching, Learning & Culture at Texas A&M University, and PI of the CoHALE Lab (Collaborative Human-AI Learning Ecologies in STEM+C). My research program is grounded in the learning sciences and sits at the intersection of design, collaboration, and computing. I design and study learning ecologies in which students and teachers develop disciplinary computational practices and high-quality collaboration, and I ask what a technology – increasingly AI – must carry, and what must stay with people, for that learning to hold up in the settings where it has to run: everyday classrooms, teacher preparation, and professional development.

I co-design with teachers and study enactment in authentic settings through design-based research with mixed methods. Across projects, I build competence models into single artifacts that both scaffold practice and measure it, so that what a model misses becomes visible in use. I hold a Ph.D. in Learning, Design, and Technology from The Pennsylvania State University and completed postdoctoral work at the University of Pennsylvania and the University of Connecticut.

Three research strands

Funded projects →

The strands run through the whole program rather than dividing it, so every project works on more than one. Each project below appears once, tagged with the strands it addresses.

Computational practices
Disciplinary computational practices
Computation as part of a subject's own reasoning – the data an agronomist reads, the judgment a teacher makes about an AI tool. I study how computational thinking, data practice, and AI literacy take their shape from the discipline they sit in, and how students and teachers learn them outside a computer science classroom.
Collaborative competence
High-quality collaboration
What productive joint work looks like – in student teams, human-AI exchange, and teacher-researcher co-design – and whether the people doing it can recognize and improve it.
AI as participant
AI and emerging technologies as participants in the ecology
What a technology must carry, and what must stay with people. Platforms that return to teams the criteria they're judged by; sandboxes that make rules executable; AI mentors designed as coaching plus reflection, scaffolding inquiry without handing thinking over.

NSF CAREER · PI · 2026–2031
3AforCT: AI-guided Ag-STEM inquiry in rural middle schools
Rural middle schoolers investigate their own school gardens with sensors, carrying data practices across the full inquiry cycle. An AI peer mentor models expert reasoning and fades as teams take it over, and teachers co-design the sequence they will teach.
